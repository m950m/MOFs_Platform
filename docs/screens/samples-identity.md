# Samples & identity

The screen that protects the core scientific distinction: **the reported
tested sample** — not a generic material name — is what evidence belongs to.

## What enters

1. **A reported sample** (per source): designation, parent framework as
   cited, linker, metal node, composition, additions (e.g. nanoparticles),
   structure reference (CIF/model as cited), activation / pretreatment, and
   its **basis** — `experimental` / `computational` / `hypothetical`.
2. **Lineage (only if documented)** — mark a sample as `composite` or
   `derived` *of* another recorded sample.
3. **Observations** — attach to one sample only: kind
   (experimental / computational / hypothetical), value, unit, reaction
   (HER / OER / other / unknown), medium/electrolyte, reference/conversion,
   loading, duration, protocol, and the evidence location (or `unknown`).
4. **Operating states** — what the sample was before/during/after the
   reaction, with the author's phase interpretation and its epistemic type.
5. **Comparisons** — pick two samples and compare them under the identity
   contract.

## What comes out

- Sample cards with their observations and operating states.
- Comparison results — a **scoped relation**: `same reported sample` /
  `same parent framework` / `derived` / `composite` / `different` /
  `unresolved`, at an entity level, with the reason, merge permission, review
  state, and evidence location.
- The recorded relations list.

## Honest markers

- **Observations belong to their sample only.** They never appear on a parent
  framework or a hypothesized phase.
- **No relation ever merges records.** Comparing two samples links them with
  a reasoned relation; the rows stay separate.
- The same name, formula, DOI, MOFid, or CIF alone **never** proves the same
  tested sample — identity is a human-reviewed relation, not a metadata match.
- An author's active-phase interpretation never silently renames the prepared
  sample.
- Missing condition fields are honest `unknown`s, shown as gaps — never
  compared or ranked.
